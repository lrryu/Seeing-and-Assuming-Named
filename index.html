01

The measurement problem

Output-level evaluation sees the emitted label. A model that changes most of its labels when an image is added appears visual across the whole city, even where its internal state stays close to the text-only baseline. The same label can arise from different internal balances between the street scene and the district name.

The audit reads that balance at each location. It contrasts the model's penultimate residual-stream state under image-plus-text against the text-only baseline, and projects the displacement onto an axis defined by the model's answer partition. The result is a continuous reading of how far the visual scene moves the internal representation beyond the district prior.

Nested samples support the analysis: 5,000 street-view locations across the nine districts of Shenzhen, and a 668-point subsample evaluated across three open-weight architectures and six visual conditions. The classification task uses a twelve-class answer space: urban land-use classes plus an Other category.

Study design

The 668-point mechanism subsample sits inside the 5,000-point city-wide sample. The condition matrix crosses image state with the presence of the district name. G1–G4 define the endpoints used at scale; G5–G6 and the blur and grayscale arms test intermediate degradation on the subsample.

The six conditions share the observation site, prompt syntax, model weights and decoding settings, so invariant context cancels in the paired state difference.

02

Behavioural asymmetry

Adding an image changes 84.6% of Qwen3-VL-8B labels when the district name is present, and 83.8% when it is absent. Removing the district name while the sharp image is held constant changes 3.3%. Judged by emitted labels alone, the district prompt appears inert once an image is supplied.

The three architectures operate under different prior-interaction regimes. LLaVA-1.6-7B changes 92.8% of labels when the district name is present and 9.7% when it is absent; InternVL2-8B changes 52.2% and 52.5% in both arms.

Withholding the image separates the models further. In Qwen3-VL-8B, predictions collapse into a small set of default categories that partition along district boundaries, and restoring the image redistributes them across the twelve classes.

03

Internal depth

Across transformer layers, the projected answer moves toward the emitted label. At the final layer, agreement between the logit-lens projection and the emitted class is 53.4% in Qwen3-VL-8B, 18.3% in LLaVA-1.6-7B and 1.9% in InternVL2-8B. The with-minus-no state difference grows with depth up to the penultimate layer in all three architectures.

Layer indices run from the embedding output (0) to layer 36 in Qwen3-VL-8B and layer 32 in LLaVA-1.6-7B and InternVL2-8B. These readings answer different questions: alignment asks when the projection matches the output; the state difference asks where image and text conditions separate.

04

The spatial field

Projected onto the municipal road network, the reading forms a field with structure at two scales. District names set a district-wide baseline; within the same district and predicted class, visual displacement still clusters along the road network.

The conditioning sequence removes one composition at a time. The raw field is autocorrelated (Moran's I = +0.632, p = 0.001). Removing district means leaves +0.584; removing predicted answer-class means leaves +0.351; removing both leaves +0.090 (p = 0.006) on the 1,473 connected road-network locations. The fully residualised field is the one used in the urban-form analysis.

4,931 of the 5,000 locations carry road-network support. The remaining 69 appear in grey and are excluded from the network statistics.

06

Urban form

Visual displacement is larger on open through-roads and smaller in dense, point-of-interest-rich contexts. The density gradient is estimated on the 4,785 classified road-linked locations; the district coefficients carry the same specification within each administrative district.

Cochran's Q homogeneity places the between-district heterogeneity at I² = 6.2% for through-roads, 20.4% for log POI and 34.6% for built density. Within-class network Moran by district ranges from −0.219 in Yantian to +0.372 in Longhua, against a pooled estimate of +0.090.

Targeted audit

Within predicted Residential (101), high-residual locations disagree with the EULUC-Globe reference at 49.0%, against 36.6% for the full residential class — a difference of +12.4 percentage points. Post-stratifying across residual quartiles returns the class-wide estimate.

Across answer classes, the enrichment is concentrated in 101: +16.9 pp with Fisher p = 1.8 × 10⁻⁴. The other classes in Table S12 show differences between −1.2 and +3.2 pp. In the hybrid design that allocates half the inspection effort to the top quartile and half across the remaining quartiles, post-stratification returns 41.1 disagreements per 100 inspections.
